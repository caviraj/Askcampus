# Examination Handbook 2026-27

--- page 1 ---

## 1. Examination fee

The examination fee for the odd semester is 1,200 rupees for undergraduate
programmes and 1,500 rupees for postgraduate programmes. The fee is paid through
the student portal and is collected by the Examination Cell.

The due date for the odd semester examination fee is 5 December 2026. A student
who has not paid by that date will not be issued a hall ticket until the fee and
any late fee are cleared.

## 2. Late payment

A late fee of 250 rupees applies to any examination fee paid after the due date.
The late fee is a flat charge; it does not increase with the number of days the
payment is late.

The last date on which a late payment is accepted is 12 December 2026. After that
date the student is treated as not registered for the examination and must apply
for the supplementary examination instead.

--- page 2 ---

## 3. Hall tickets

Hall tickets are released on the student portal on 20 December 2026. A hall
ticket is required for every examination; a student without one will not be
admitted to the examination hall.

A hall ticket carries the student's name, register number, programme, and the
seating arrangement for each paper. A printing error should be reported to the
Examination Cell at least two days before the first paper.

## 4. Revaluation

A student may apply for revaluation of an answer sheet within 10 days of the
result being published. The revaluation fee is 500 rupees per answer sheet.

Revaluation is available only for theory papers. Practical examinations,
internal assessments, and project viva marks cannot be revalued. The outcome of
a revaluation is final and is published within 30 days of the application.

## 5. Supplementary examinations

A supplementary examination is held in March for students who failed a paper or
who could not appear for the regular examination. The supplementary examination
fee is 400 rupees per paper.

## 6. Malpractice

Copying in the examination hall, carrying an unauthorised device, or exchanging
answer sheets is treated as malpractice. A student found to have committed
malpractice is barred from the remaining papers of that semester.

The penalty for a first instance of malpractice is the cancellation of all
papers written in that semester. A second instance leads to suspension for one
academic year.
